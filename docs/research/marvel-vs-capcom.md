# Marvel vs. Capcom: what makes it work, and what After Hours takes from it

Research notes for the tag-team overhaul. We take the genre's ideas (tag teams, assists, launchers,
team supers, stacked meter), never its characters, names, logos or art.

## Gameplay

- **Teams, not duels.** You pick a team (three in MvC2/3, two in MvC1 and Infinite) and can tag in
  a partner at any time. Commentators call the tag system "the heart and soul of the game's depth":
  you manage the team, choose who comes in next, and read the opponent's choices.
- **Assists.** Each character's partner can be called in for one move (MvC2 lets you pick one of
  three assist types per character). Assists are *punishable*: the opponent can hit the partner
  while it is on screen, which is the risk that balances the reward. Assists plus quick links are
  what make "over-the-top combos" possible.
- **Hyper combos and team supers.** A shared hyper meter fills in levels (up to 5 in MvC3); a
  hyper costs a level. "Delayed hyper combos" chain one partner's hyper into the next, and
  "variable combinations" bring the whole team in for one super together.
- **Launcher and air combo.** Nearly every character has a launcher that knocks the opponent high
  into the air; you super jump after them and finish with an air combo. The basic shape: three
  ground hits (light, medium, heavy: the "magic series" of chains), launcher, about four air hits.
- **Red health.** A partner resting on the bench slowly gets back part of the damage it took,
  which rewards tagging out a hurt fighter.
- **Comeback mechanic.** MvC3's X-Factor (all four attack buttons) powers up the last fighters
  for a short time.

## UI

- Each fighter has their own life bar; the point character's is the biggest and highest, the
  partners' are smaller beneath it. The shared hyper meter sits at the bottom with a level
  number. Skullgirls' designers warn that the super bar must show its *level* clearly, and that
  health and recoverable health need distinct colours.
- Character select: MvC2 is praised for showing the whole roster at once with splash art, a
  moving model and a great portrait for each.

## What players love, and what went wrong

- Loved: speed, spectacle, huge combos, team building and "how do these two work together", the
  comic-book energy of MvC2's presentation.
- MvC Infinite was criticised for its presentation: art that "lost much of the comic book magic",
  weaker character designs, reused animations and a thin roster. Its gameplay was often praised.
  Lesson: the characters must look great and distinct.

## What we take

| MvC idea | After Hours version |
|---|---|
| Teams with tag-ins | 2-on-2 teams: pick a point fighter and a partner. Tag (Block + Special) swaps them; the partner jumps in. |
| Assists | Partner Assist (Block + Light): the partner runs in, throws their projectile special, and leaves. They can be hit while out. Cooldown between calls. |
| Red health | The benched partner slowly recovers part of their lost health. |
| Hyper levels | The hyper meter now holds 3 levels; a super costs one. |
| Team super | A super with Block held brings the partner in for theirs right after (costs a second level). |
| Launcher + air combo | Up + Heavy is a launcher on every fighter; after it, jumping attacks chain (light into heavy) in the air. |
| Comeback | We already have Rally (half-cost supers when low) and Heat. |
| One-round matches | A tag match is one round: knock out both fighters to win. |
| UI | Stacked team bars with partner portraits, assist-ready light, hyper levels 1-3 with a big number; team select picks two. |
| Distinct, great-looking characters | Every fighter rebuilt from their character sheet for a real likeness, plus two original fantasy fighters. |
